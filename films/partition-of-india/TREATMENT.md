## 1. Logline and controlling idea

**Logline.** In the summer of 1947 a line was drawn across the Punjab and Bengal from a six-year-old census, by a chairman who never sat in the room, and published the day after two nations were born; the film follows that line from the census table to the roads, in the words of the people who argued it, signed it, held it, crossed it and counted its dead.

**Controlling idea.** The record is exact about the line, down to the tahsil boundary and the river, and inexact about the people it cut through, whom it holds only as counts that disagree; the film shows the line at the record's precision and the people at the record's silence, and lets each side say its own number.

### 2.1 How a reading's picture is chosen (binding for this film; proposed as grammar amendment A8)

In order; the first that holds is used, and the reviewer checks each reading against this list.

1. **The speaker, at the time of the words.** S-008, Jinnah at the All India Radio microphones on 3 June, would carry Jinnah's 3 June broadcast.
2. **The speaker's own occasion:** the same place in the same week, the speaker in frame. S-009 (Karachi, 14 August) carries Jinnah's address of 11 August to the same Assembly; the narrator gives both dates.
3. **The same place and period, or the same subject in the same period** (the period is the film's span, 1946 to 1949): a photograph whose caption places it where the words are about. The narrator's one-sentence introduction then gives the photograph's own place and date as its caption gives them, so the viewer is never told it is the speaker's exact scene unless it is. The mark and the credit stay on screen for the whole reading.
4. **The speaker's own portrait from another year**, the mark carrying that year (S-016 for Baldev Singh, 1950; S-013 for Liaquat Ali Khan, 1945).
5. **A DRAWN map or typeset page of the places or figures the reading names**, with the reading's own extract as the data source (Gurdaspur's tahsils under Din Mohammad; the recovery figures under the Chief Liaison Officer).
6. **Black**, only where the absence is the meaning: the chairman's award in the cold open, each count of the dead, the poem, and any pending account for which no picture passes 1 to 5.

Never: a photograph of a named person other than the speaker, unless the words are about that person or that event and the narrator says so; a column, camp or crowd whose caption names one community under a voice from another; a photograph that could be taken for the speaker's specific event when it is not, without the narrator naming its caption; a partisan government's statement over any photograph (it is read over a DRAWN typeset page that carries the pamphlet's title and publisher, under its perspective card); a print whose date is later than the scene without the mark carrying the scene's year as the ledger records it (the four Photo Division stills once catalogued 1954-02, S-037, S-038, S-042 and S-043, are dated 1947 in the ledger with `date_catalogued` kept, and carry 1947). A pending slot that fails is a record-is-silent card of 5 s, never a black hold; a chapter joins its failed slots into one card.
