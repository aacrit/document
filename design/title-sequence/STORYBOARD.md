# void document · As It Was: storyboard

12 beats, 57.0 s, 1,368 frames at 24 fps. One continuous camera through beats 1 to 11, then a cut to the record, a dip and the lockup. Style frames: `frames/01-clay.html` to `frames/12-lockup.html`, all on one page in `index.html`. Sources and ledger ids: `ASSETS.md`.

## Motion vocabulary (BRAND-KIT 6, 11, 13; nothing new)

| Name | What it does here |
|---|---|
| `rostrum` | **The camera, always.** Velocity ramps on a smoothstep over min(0.9 s, 0.3 of the move), then cruises at constant speed: a motion-control rig, never a spring. Chained between beats, so one cruise speed hands over to the next through a ramp, never a stop. |
| `arrive` | The largest object in a beat rises into its place (zeta 0.909, 0.565 s, overshoot 0.11%) |
| `settle` | A gear engages, a board lands, the machine comes to rest (0.450 s) |
| `stamp` | A platen, a key, a typebar or the census press makes contact (0.341 s, no overshoot: it presses, it never bounces) |
| `trace` | A slow travel the eye follows: a board unfolding, the lamp's glint along Ino's survey line (1.56 s) |
| `drift` | The logomark's corners close (0.934 s) |
| `whisper` / `let go` | The provenance labels (BRAND-KIT 12): surface on `whisper`, hold 2.0 s, let go over 0.75 s |
| constant | Once engaged, every wheel turns at constant speed, like the credit roll: "a projector does not ease" |

Arrivals are springs and departures are fades: nothing slides out. The house springs overshoot by less than 0.2%, so nothing visibly bounces.

### 1. Clay · 0:00.00 to 0:05.00 · frames 0 to 119

- **Artifact.** The record of temple workers, Walters Art Museum 48.1767. One of the largest surviving Neo-Sumerian tablets: 24 columns naming nearly 20,000 temple workers of the Umma area, from the 37th year of Shulgi. It is a register of people, the clay ancestor of every census in the machine.
- **Picture.** Black. The obverse emerges grain by grain as a raking key finds its wedges. The tablet is set edge-true into a fired-clay ring with 24 teeth, one per column. Across the frame the ring meshes with a second ring.
- **Camera.** It holds still for 1.0 s, then makes a `rostrum` crane up and back (2.8 m to 3.4 m at object scale, 4.0 s). The ring is revealed turning.
- **Motion.** The lamp sweeps across the face on `drift`, from the left edge to the centre, before anything moves. Then the ring engages on `settle` (from frame 50, 0:02.08) and turns at constant speed: 15 degrees a second, one column every second.
- **Light.** One hard key at 8 degrees grazing from frame left, as cuneiform is photographed. No fill. Dust in the beam (volumetric, procedural).
- **Transition to 2.** The crane continues up into the first *station pose*: straight down over the Turin papyrus, which lies beyond the ring's rim. The ring's rim rolls onto the papyrus's edge.
- **Sound.** Silence for 12 frames, then the motif's sustained instrument alone (a bowed low string), entering under the lamp's sweep. One struck-string note as the ring engages (frame 50).
- **Mark.** At frame 0 the DRAWN glyph (cold-blue square) and `Drawn · from period records` surface. The label lets go by 0:03.11, and the glyph stays through beat 11's machine. The watermark is top right.
- **Frame.** `frames/01-clay.html`, at 0:01.50.

### 2. Atlas · 0:05.00 to 0:11.00 · frames 120 to 263

- **Artifact.** The Turin papyrus map of the Wadi Hammamat (Museo Egizio, Cat. 1879, 1899 and 1969, Ramesses IV, c. 1156 to 1150 BCE) leads to the Catalan Atlas (Abraham Cresques, Majorca, 1375; BnF Espagnol 30). The Atlas was made as panels mounted on boards, folding like a screen.
- **Picture.** First a station pass over the papyrus: the scribe's painted hills rise a few millimetres, as painted, and no higher. Then the Atlas's boards unfold one after another across the table. On the Europe and Mediterranean boards, compass roses and rhumb lines radiate like spokes. The painted mountain ranges rise from the vellum.
- **Camera.** A `rostrum` pass at the station pose (1.2 s), then a tilt from top-down to 50 degrees and a track left along the boards.
- **Motion.** Each board's hinge opens on `trace` (1.56 s, 37 frames) from frame 140, the boards 8 frames apart, landing at frames 177, 185, 193 and 201. The relief grows with the unfold.
- **Light.** The lamp is top right, at 40 degrees. The boards facing it bloom, and gilt catches.
- **Transition to 3.** The camera tracks past the last board's western margin, blank vellum and the board's wooden edge. Beyond it, a level lower in the dark, lies another sheet.
- **Sound.** The motif's rising figure, first statement: four notes, one per board landing (frames 177, 185, 193, 201), on a struck string left to ring. The drone continues.
- **Mark.** The glyph only.
- **Frame.** `frames/02-atlas.html`, at 0:08.50.

### 3. Khipu · 0:11.00 to 0:16.00 · frames 264 to 383

- **Artifact.** The Piri Reis map (Topkapı Palace Museum Library, H 1824, 1513), with the western coast of a continent the Atlas does not show. Above it, an Inka khipu (Cleveland Museum of Art 1940.469, c. 1400 to 1532), a record kept in knotted cords.
- **Picture.** The camera drops over the Atlas's edge onto the Piri Reis sheet, then up into the dark. The khipu's primary cord is wound on the rim of a slowly turning drum, and its pendant cords hang in a curtain with their knot clusters at their places.
- **Camera.** A `rostrum` descent over the edge (1.5 s), then a slow push along the curtain of cords, then a tilt down one pendant.
- **Motion.** The drum turns at constant speed. Its pendants sway a little from the turn, never more than their own length allows.
- **Light.** Backlight from above and behind. The cords are rim-lit, and the cotton glows at its edges.
- **Transition to 4.** The tilt down one pendant cord: its two-ply twist becomes the thread of the press's wooden screw, matched in scale and direction.
- **Sound.** Plucked strings (pizzicato, never percussion) in clusters, like knots: sparse, uneven, not a rhythm. The drone moves up a fifth.
- **Mark.** The glyph only.
- **Frame.** `frames/03-khipu.html`, at 0:14.00.

### 4. Press · 0:16.00 to 0:20.50 · frames 384 to 491

- **Artifact.** The opening of Genesis in the 42-line Gutenberg Bible (Mainz, c. 1455), with its red rubric. The common press is modelled after Jost Amman's woodcut "Der Buchdrucker" (1568) because no press of Gutenberg's own survives.
- **Picture.** The camera travels down the screw to the bed. The printed leaf lies under the platen, which comes down.
- **Camera.** A `rostrum` descent along the screw (1.6 s), then a low track across the leaf toward the platen.
- **Motion.** The bar pulls, and the platen lands on `stamp` at 0:18.00 (frame 432) and lifts on a fade of its own shadow, not a move. After contact the leaf is lit for the first time.
- **Light.** A side key from frame right, low. The platen's shadow lies across the leaf until the stamp.
- **Transition to 5.** The leaf is drawn off the bed. The camera follows it up and over, and it lands face down. Paper on paper, its back gives way to the next sheet: a station pass top-down onto Ino's survey.
- **Sound.** The stamp lands on a low struck string (frame 432), and the motif restates an octave higher.
- **Mark.** The glyph only.
- **Frame.** `frames/04-press.html`, at 0:18.00.

### 5. Survey · 0:20.50 to 0:25.50 · frames 492 to 611

- **Artifact.** A sheet of Ino Tadataka's *Dai Nihon enkai yochi zenzu*, the large map of Japan's coast, roads and rivers from his surveys of 1800 to 1816 (Library of Congress 2001620020, catalogued [1821]). It is manuscript, watercolour and ink on rice paper, with relief shown pictorially and survey traverse lines in red.
- **Picture.** Low over the sheet, which recedes to its edge. The pictorial mountains rise from the paper. A glint of the lamp runs along the red traverse line, as a surveyor walked it.
- **Camera.** A `rostrum` glide, low and fast for the sequence, the camera's highest cruise speed, following the glint.
- **Motion.** The glint travels on `trace` along the line's printed path, never off it.
- **Light.** The lamp is low and raking from beyond the sheet, through the rice paper's fibres.
- **Transition to 6.** At the sheet's edge the traverse line ends and a wire continues it. The camera follows the wire off the paper, through the dark, to a key.
- **Sound.** A long legato line in the upper strings rising with the glint: the motif's figure, augmented.
- **Mark.** The glyph only.
- **Frame.** `frames/05-survey.html`, at 0:23.00.

### 6. Wire · 0:25.50 to 0:29.50 · frames 612 to 707

- **Artifact.** The Morse-Vail key of 1844 (NMAH 1096762) and a telegraph register of 1849 (NMAH 706500). The tape is the real one: Morse's first message, Washington to Baltimore, 24 May 1844 (Library of Congress, Morse Papers, mcc.019).
- **Picture.** The wire reaches the key. The register's clockwork turns, and the tape runs out between its rollers with the 1844 embossing on it, then curls toward the camera.
- **Camera.** A `rostrum` push along the wire to the key, then a pull back with the tape as it emerges.
- **Motion.** The key presses on `stamp` (frame 640). The register's train starts on `settle` and the tape feeds at constant speed. The embossing on the tape is the scan, not keyed by our key, so no message is invented and the music never spells Morse.
- **Light.** A tight pin of the lamp on the stylus and rollers. Brass glints. Everything else falls off fast.
- **Transition to 7.** The tape runs on into the census press. The camera follows it into the machine, and the dial bank rises behind.
- **Sound.** The machine's pulse begins in the low strings: an ostinato at the register's feed rate. It is a pulse, not a code.
- **Mark.** The glyph only.
- **Frame.** `frames/06-wire.html`, at 0:27.50.

### 7. Count · 0:29.50 to 0:33.50 · frames 708 to 803

- **Artifact.** Herman Hollerith's electric tabulating machine of the 1890 United States census (Scientific American, 30 August 1890; Computer History Museum replica). A press reads a card by pins, and a bank of 40 clock-face counters advances.
- **Picture.** The bank of 40 dials in four rows, brass bezels and paper faces. A blank census card in the press. The pin box comes down.
- **Camera.** A `rostrum` crane up the face of the dial bank, then a push toward one dial.
- **Motion.** The press comes down on `stamp` (frame 756). Several dials advance by one division on `settle`. The machine counts one. The card stays blank: the 1890 card carried no printing, and a punched pattern would be an invented return. The dials never show a figure.
- **Light.** The lamp from top left across the dial faces, and a glint along each bezel as it advances.
- **Transition to 8.** The push into one dial: its hub and hands rhyme with the hub and spokes of a spool. Through the face, the camera finds the cinématographe's spool, matched in centre and scale.
- **Sound.** The press lands on a struck string (frame 756). The ostinato continues, and the motif's figure passes to the cellos.
- **Mark.** The glyph only.
- **Frame.** `frames/07-count.html`, at 0:31.50.

### 8. Reel · 0:33.50 to 0:37.50 · frames 804 to 899

- **Artifact.** The Lumière cinématographe, camera and projector in one box (1896). The film is the real one: Alexandre Promio's *Panorama du Grand Canal pris d'un bateau*, Venice, October to December 1896, shot from a moving boat and the first travelling shot, here inside ours. Lumière film has one round perforation on each side of each frame.
- **Picture.** The spool on top of the box turns. The strip runs down into the gate, and its small frames of the Grand Canal pass by. The lamp, outside the box as the Lumières' lantern was, shines through the gate, and its beam opens into haze.
- **Camera.** A `rostrum` track down the strip from the spool to the gate, then a turn to look along the beam.
- **Motion.** The spool turns at constant speed. The strip advances through the gate intermittently, as the claw did, frame by frame (16 a second, the cinématographe's rate).
- **Light.** The beam is the lamp. Dust drifts in the volume (procedural). The box is lit only by the light that leaks from the gate.
- **Transition to 9.** The strip leaves the gate and runs on. Its edge curls around a black roller, the Underwood's platen, and the strip gives way to a sheet of paper.
- **Sound.** The ostinato lightens (strings, sul tasto). The motif sounds once at full, the sequence's widest moment.
- **Mark.** The glyph only. The frames inside the strip are textures inside the model and do not change the mark.
- **Frame.** `frames/08-reel.html`, at 0:35.50.

### 9. Type · 0:37.50 to 0:41.50 · frames 900 to 995

- **Artifact.** The Underwood Standard No. 5, introduced in 1900, a front-strike machine whose 42 typebars rest in a semicircular basket. The paper is a real typed page of 1900 to 1931 (source to be fixed in `ASSETS.md`, T-18).
- **Picture.** The basket's fan of bars, low and close. One bar rises out of the fan and strikes the page on the platen. Above, the typed lines are already there.
- **Camera.** A `rostrum` push low across the fan toward the print point.
- **Motion.** A typebar strikes on `stamp` (frame 948) and falls back on a fade of its motion blur. The carriage steps once. The page's real text is revealed one character at a time along its own lines, the real page shown at typing speed with no letter of ours.
- **Light.** A low side key from frame right. A glint runs along each bar's edge.
- **Transition to 10.** The page rolls up over the platen. The camera rises with it and over the top, and the sheet becomes a printed sea chart below: a station pass, top-down.
- **Sound.** A struck string on the strike (frame 948). The ostinato begins to thin.
- **Mark.** The glyph only.
- **Frame.** `frames/09-type.html`, at 0:39.50.

### 10. Swell · 0:41.50 to 0:46.00 · frames 996 to 1103

- **Artifact.** A rebbelib (Library of Congress 2010586182, 1920s): a Marshallese stick chart of both the Ratak and Ralik chains. Shells mark the atolls, straight sticks the regular currents and curved sticks the ocean swells. Typed paper strips with atoll names are glued to its sticks. Below it lies Admiralty chart 983, *Marshall Islands* (published 1891, the new edition of 1931).
- **Picture.** The printed chart spreads in the lamp's pool. The rebbelib rises above it on `arrive`, and its shadow falls across the printed atolls: two charts of one sea, one laid over the other by light alone.
- **Camera.** A station pass top-down onto the chart, then a slow `rostrum` crane up as the stick chart rises between the camera and the chart.
- **Motion.** The rebbelib arrives on `arrive` and holds, the sequence's only stillness before the end. The machine's constant motions begin to slow, each wheel coming to rest on `settle` in the order the camera met them.
- **Light.** The lamp is high, at top left, a hard source, so the shadow of the sticks is sharp on the printed chart.
- **Transition to 11.** The lamp's pool lets go of the chart (a fade). The camera rises into the dark, and the lamp, rising with it, finds one plate.
- **Sound.** The ostinato stops. The drone alone. The motif's figure begins its last statement, unfinished.
- **Mark.** The glyph only.
- **Frame.** `frames/10-swell.html`, at 0:43.50.

### 11. Record · 0:46.00 to 0:52.00 · frames 1104 to 1247

- **Artifact.** The film's own record, from its own ledger. For the Partition film: *The Gazette of India Extraordinary*, Sunday 17 August 1947, page 1 (S-130). Others are in `CONCEPT.md`.
- **Picture, 0:46.00 to 0:48.00 (machine).** The machine is at rest. The camera arrives frontal on a plate textured with the record, held in the last wheel's frame. The lamp comes to rest on it.
- **Cut, 0:48.00 (frame 1152).** A hard cut to the real scan, whole, pillarboxed on black (7.2 HOLD, A7). The plate's final pose registers exactly with the HOLD framing, so the cut reads as the model giving way to the record, not as a jump.
- **Camera.** The `rostrum` ramps down to rest on the matched pose at frame 1151. After the cut there is no move.
- **Light.** On the model, the lamp settles from raking to frontal. After the cut, the scan as it is: no grade, no tint.
- **Transition to 12.** A dip to black, 0.75 s centred on 0:52.00. The fade-out runs from 0:51.625, the hold is 2 frames of black (A28), and the fade-in runs to 0:52.375. ARCHIVE to CARD, so a dip, never a dissolve.
- **Sound.** On the cut, the music resolves to the motif's last note and holds it, a struck string left to ring over the drone. Under the record nothing new begins.
- **Mark.** At the cut the glyph turns to ARCHIVE's amber dot, a 4-frame linear crossfade at the class change. `Archive · 1947` surfaces on `whisper`, holds 2.0 s and lets go by 0:51.11. This is the first safelight of the sequence. The watermark fades out linearly over frames 1236 to 1247, before the CARD.
- **Frame.** `frames/11-record.html` (the ARCHIVE hold at 0:49.00; switch films in the frame's controls).

### 12. Lockup · 0:52.00 to 0:57.00 · frames 1248 to 1367

- **Picture.** True black (CARD). The logomark and, on one line, `void document · As It Was`: `void` in JetBrains Mono 500, `document` in Fraunces 300, a middle dot, `As It Was` in Fraunces 300 italic (the series is a title of a work, and set as one). It is centred, at 5.6% of frame height (60 px at 1080p), the chapter title's size. The logomark is the same size, with clear space of one ring diameter all round.
- **Motion.** From 0:52.375 (frame 1257) the logomark's four corners close from 8% outside their rest on `drift` (23 frames) while the strokes fade in with the same progress, and the ember appears in the last tenth: the watermark's own settle, here at full size. The words come 0.4 s later on `settle`, rising 0.6% of frame height (the title card's grammar). Hold. Out: everything fades together over the last 16 frames (0:56.33 to 0:57.00, ease-in-quad). Nothing moves on the way out.
- **Light.** None but the type. Bone strokes and words, and the safelight ember, which is its second and last appearance.
- **Transition.** Black, then the film's title card (CARD to CARD, a cut), then Part 1.
- **Sound.** The motif's rising figure completes as the corners close, landing its last note with the ember (frame 1278). It rings out under the hold and is gone by the fade's end.
- **Mark.** The CARD ring, bottom right. No watermark (a CARD).
- **Frame.** `frames/12-lockup.html`, at 0:54.50.
