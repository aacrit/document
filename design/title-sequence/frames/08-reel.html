Artifact
The Lumière cinématographe, camera and projector in one box (1896). The film is the real one: Alexandre Promio's Panorama du Grand Canal pris d'un bateau, Venice, 1896, shot from a moving boat: the first travelling shot, inside ours. Lumière film has one round perforation each side of each frame.
Picture
The spool on top turns; the strip runs through the gate and its small Grand Canal frames pass; the lamp, outside the box as the Lumières' lantern was, shines through the gate and its beam opens into haze.
Camera
A rostrum track down the strip from the spool to the gate, then a turn to look along the beam.
Light
The beam is the lamp; dust drifts in it. The box is lit only by what leaks from the gate.
Motion
The spool turns at constant speed; the strip advances through the gate intermittently, frame by frame, 16 a second, as the claw did.
Transition
The strip runs on out of the gate; its edge curls around a black roller, the Underwood's platen, and gives way to a sheet of paper.
Sound
The ostinato lightens (strings, sul tasto); the motif sounds once at full, the widest moment of the sequence.
Mark
The DRAWN glyph only. The frames on the strip are a texture inside the model and do not change the mark. Promio's gondoliers are never framed as subjects.
